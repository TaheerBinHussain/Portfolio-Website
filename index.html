Independent research, April 2026

Self-directed study, not a published paper

From reactive to predictive autoscaling in Kubernetes

Can predictive autoscaling using ARIMA forecasts improve Kubernetes latency and resource efficiency compared with the reactive Horizontal Pod Autoscaler?

Method

I built a custom predictive autoscaling controller that forecasts load with ARIMA and scales ahead of demand, then compared it with Kubernetes' built-in reactive HPA across 40 controlled experiments driven by k6.

  1. k6 load generator
  2. Service on Kubernetes
  3. Prometheus metrics
  4. ARIMA controller vs reactive HPA

Workload patterns

  • Steady
  • Burst
  • Ramp
  • Spiky

Measured

p95 latency, error rate, scaling latency, CPU-replica-seconds, over-provisioning ratio

But under sudden bursts

Reactive HPA performed better. That fits how the two approaches work: a forecast built from recent history has no warning of a sudden spike, while HPA reacts to the load that is actually arriving.

Limitations

  • The predictive gains held for steady load, not for sudden bursts.
  • Results come from 40 controlled runs in my own test setup and haven't been peer-reviewed.
